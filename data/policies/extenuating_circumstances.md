# Academic Regulations: Extenuating Circumstances & Coursework Extension Policy 2026

**Document Code**: REG-2026-EC  
**Authoritative Body**: University Academic Registrar & Education Committee  
**Target Audience**: All Undergraduate, Postgraduate Taught, and Postgraduate Research Students  
**Effective Date**: Academic Year 2026/2027  

---

### §1.0 Purpose and Scope
This policy sets out the principles and procedures under which students may request an extension to an assessment submission deadline or deferral of an examination due to sudden, unforeseen, and unpreventable circumstances outside their control.

---

### §2.0 Definition of Extenuating Circumstances
Extenuating circumstances (also referred to as mitigating circumstances) are defined as genuine, acute, and serious personal or medical events that temporarily impair a student's capacity to complete coursework or examinations by the scheduled deadline.

**Recognized Valid Circumstances**:
1. **Acute Illness or Injury**: Serious medical conditions, sudden hospitalization, or debilitating physical or psychiatric flare-ups.
2. **Bereavement**: The recent death or critical illness of an immediate family member, guardian, partner, or close personal dependant.
3. **Severe Personal Adversity**: Being the victim of a serious crime, domestic crisis, sudden homelessness, or acute financial catastrophe.
4. **Disability/Mental Health Exacerbation**: Sudden, unmanaged exacerbation of an existing condition where normal accommodations prove temporarily insufficient.

---

### §3.0 Non-Eligible Circumstances (Circumstances NOT Permitted)
The university does not accept the following as grounds for extenuating circumstances:
* Normal academic stress, general examination anxiety, or heavy study workload.
* Poor time management, misreading the examination timetable, or overlapping assessment dates.
* Failure of personal technology (e.g., laptop crashes, uncharged batteries) without documented IT support logs.
* Routine social, domestic, or travel commitments (e.g., family holidays, weddings, employment shifts).

---

### §4.1 Self-Certification Rules
Students may submit a **Self-Certification Extension Request** without providing third-party medical evidence under the following strict conditions:
* **Duration**: Grants a standard **5-calendar-day extension** from the original assessment deadline.
* **Annual Allowance**: Students may use self-certification a maximum of **two (2) times per academic year**.
* **Exclusions**: Self-certification cannot be applied to in-person timed examinations, laboratory practical assessments, or final dissertation/thesis viva deadlines.

---

### §4.2 Application Procedure and Deadlines
1. **Submission Window**: Applications must be lodged via the Student Wellbeing Portal **at least 48 hours prior** to the published assessment deadline.
2. **Late Applications**: Applications submitted after the deadline (up to 7 calendar days late) are only considered in exceptional circumstances where the student was physically incapacitated or medically hospitalized.
3. **Notification of Outcome**: Official decisions are communicated to the student's institutional email address within **3 working days** of submission.

---

### §4.3 Third-Party Evidence Requirements
For extension requests exceeding 5 days, or where self-certification allowances have been exhausted, students must provide verifiable third-party documentation:
* **Medical Evidence**: Medical certificate signed by a licensed General Practitioner (GP), hospital discharge summary, or registered mental health specialist note.
* **Evidence Dating**: Evidence must explicitly state that the student was incapacitated during the specific preparation or submission window of the assessment.
* **Language**: Documents in languages other than English must be accompanied by a certified, accredited translation.

---

### §5.0 Outcomes and Standard Extension Allowances
* **Standard Extension**: Between **5 to 10 working days**, agreed in consultation with the Module Leader.
* **Longer Extension**: In severe clinical cases, up to a maximum of **14 working days**.
* **Assessment Deferral**: If an extension of 14 days is clinically insufficient, the board will recommend an **Assessment Deferral** to the next available examination window without academic penalty.
