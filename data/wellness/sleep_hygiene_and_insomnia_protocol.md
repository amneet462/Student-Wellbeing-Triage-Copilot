# Sleep Hygiene and Circadian Stabilization for Academic Stress

**Clinical Framework**: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I)  
**Target Symptoms**: Nighttime racing thoughts, exam insomnia, daytime fatigue, caffeine crashes  
**Estimated Time**: Nightly ritual  

---

### §1.0 The Sleep-Anxiety Hyperarousal Cycle
Sleep deprivation directly destabilizes the emotional brain. When university students pull 'all-nighters' or sleep less than 6 hours before exams, amygdala reactivity increases by up to **60%**, while hippocampal memory consolidation drops sharply. Sleeplessness creates daytime panic, which creates bedtime hyperarousal, creating a vicious cycle.

---

### §2.0 Stimulus Control Therapy (CBT-I Gold Standard)
The human brain forms strong associative conditioned reflexes. If you spend 3 hours in bed tossing, turning, and panicking about deadlines, your brain learns: *Bed = Anxiety & Panic*.

1. **The 20-Minute Departure Rule**: If you have not fallen asleep after approximately 20 minutes in bed, **get out of bed immediately**.
2. **Move to Low-Light Seating**: Sit in a comfortable chair under dim, warm lamplight.
3. **Engage in Low-Stimulation Activity**: Read a physical book, listen to soft ambient audio, or do 5-4-3-2-1 grounding. Do NOT check university email, exam portals, or social media.
4. **Return Only When Sleepy**: When your eyelids feel heavy and you yawn, return to bed. Repeat as needed to re-associate bed with sleep.

---

### §3.0 The Pre-Sleep 'Worry Journal' (Brain Dump Protocol)
Racing thoughts at night are caused by the brain's executive memory holding onto uncompleted tasks:

* At least **60 minutes before bedtime**, sit at your study desk with a physical notebook.
* Spend 10 minutes writing out every single academic worry, deadline, and task on your mind.
* Next to each item, write one small, concrete step for tomorrow morning (e.g., "Worry: Dissertation literature review $\rightarrow$ Tomorrow 10 AM: Read abstract of Smith et al.").
* Physically close the notebook. Tell yourself: *"My responsibilities are catalogued and safe. Nighttime is for cellular restoration; thinking about them at 2 AM changes nothing."*

---

### §4.0 Night-Before-Exam Panic Protocol
* If you wake up in a sweat the night before an exam: Keep your lights off or very dim.
* Place your hands on your belly and do 10 rounds of **4-4-4-4 Box Breathing**.
* Remind yourself: *Even resting with your eyes closed in a calm state restores 80% of physiological and cognitive reserves. You do not need perfect sleep to perform well.*
